About this run and how it is drawn

A coupling test, not a nitrogen assessment. Hydrology: OS-LISFLOOD v5.0.0 with the official GloFAS v5 settings and ERA5-based forcing (JRC open data), cold-started at the first date shown. Water quality: openWQ with its basic nitrogen template (ORG_N → NH4-N → NO3-N → N2 lost, constant first-order rates), set up with openWQ's configuration generator.

Every compartment starts at 1 mg-N/L of each species. Water infiltrating the topsoil carries a fixed test load of 10 mg/L NO3-N. Solute moves with LISFLOOD's own water fluxes: soil layers, groundwater zones and cell-to-cell river routing. Lakes and reservoirs pass solute straight through. N2_LOSS is the cumulative nitrogen removed by denitrification in each compartment.

How the maps are drawn:

  • The page receives values, not colours. Each layer is coded in 254 log₁₀ steps between its 0.5th and 99.9th percentile over all dates (step size in the legend); values beyond take the end codes and read as ≤ or ≥. Your graphics card applies the colour scale, range and view you choose.
  • Topsoil and groundwater: mean of the wet model cells under each map pixel (size in the legend). Smooth pixels interpolates between neighbouring pixels that have values; switch it off to see the pixels as computed.
  • Rivers: every LISFLOOD channel cell with at least the stated upstream area, drawn from its centre to the centre of its downstream cell along the model's own routing, coloured by that cell's value. Line width grows with upstream area; smaller rivers appear as you zoom in.
  • Time: the model wrote a map every week at 00:00 UTC. Smooth time interpolates log₁₀ values linearly between two outputs, and the date panel says so; switch it off to step from output to output. Probes and statistics use the outputs only.
  • Departure: log₁₀ of the value divided by the geometric mean of all dates at the same place (×2 means twice the mean).
  • Flow direction: dashes travel from each cell to its downstream cell. Their speed is constant on screen and says nothing about flow velocity.
  • Selecting a river: a click near a drawn river snaps to its nearest channel cell. Every mapped cell draining to it lights up in the probe's colour, as do its main stem and its path to the outlet; the rest of the network steps back. The main stem follows the branch with the larger upstream area at every confluence, from where the river first drains the network threshold down to the outlet.
  • River card: Main stem, values along that path on the date shown, with the range over all outputs at each cell and the upstream area (grey, right axis), which steps up at every confluence. Over time, the selected cell on every output against the median and 10–90 % range of all mapped river cells upstream of it. vs upstream area, every mapped river cell upstream on the date shown against its own upstream area. In the topsoil and groundwater views the card reads the map pixel under each river cell.
  • Latitude × time: for every output date, the mean of the coded log₁₀ values (the geometric mean) over the pixels or river cells with a value in each 1° latitude band; departure view: against each band's own mean over all dates. Drag on it or on the time strip to move in time.
  • Terrain height: LISFLOOD's elevation map averaged to the nodes of a 0.25° mesh (0.5° on phones) and raised by the factor shown; 0 m outside the model domain. Picking, probes and rivers follow the raised surface. Set it to flat to see the sphere.
  • Tour: flies to the cell with the largest upstream area near each river's mouth (a fixed box per river; the names are the only text not taken from the files) and selects it. Every number in the caption comes from LISFLOOD's network and this run's outputs.
  • Histogram and time strip: weighted by pixel area over wet cells (rivers: network cells, equal weights). Line: median; band: 10th–90th percentile.
  • No value (hatched land, grey river): the cell holds less than the stated water depth, or the value overflowed the 16-bit output (a few nearly dry river cells per date).
  • Relief: hillshade of LISFLOOD's elevation map (×6 vertical exaggeration, contrast-stretched), drawn inside the model domain; the outline marks the edge of the model's land mask. Satellite, when offered: NASA Blue Marble Next Generation monthly composites of 2004, blended for the date shown; context only, not data of the simulated year. South of the model grid (dashed line) there is no model.
